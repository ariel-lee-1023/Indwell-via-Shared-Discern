# Inherited source notes

The original version recorded verification on 2026-10-09. These notes are retained as historical provenance, outside the five-book original-English audit. An attempt to reopen the official Issue 9 PDF during that audit returned HTTP 403; it did not verify the standard or the other external sources. Everyday teaching does not require reopening every link.

## Normative sources

1. [Official ASD-STE100 Issue 9 PDF](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf), 2025-01-15.
   - The original record describes 434 PDF pages and checks of rule summaries, relevant details, introductory material, and selected dictionary entries, not a complete dictionary audit.
   - Part 1 rule summaries: PDF pages 45, 63, 67, 77, 87, 95, 103, 107, 115.
   - Specific locations: 3.6 (PDF71–72); 4.4 (83); 5.2 (88–89); 5.4–5.5 (90–93); 6.1–6.6 (95–101). These are file-page numbers, not printed pagination.
   - Formal English compliance requires the complete official rules and dictionary; this skill offers principle-based adaptation.
2. [Official FAQ](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html), used in the original record to check technical terminology, passive-voice exceptions, training, and tool limitations. A page snapshot is not a permanently unchanged standard.
3. [Official introduction](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html), used in the original record for Issue 9, 53 rules, nine sections, and approximately 900 approved general words.
4. [Official ASD-STE100 and AI white paper](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/WhitePaper-ASD-STE100_and_AI.pdf), June 2026, three pages according to the original record.
   - The inherited account describes support for AI assistance alongside concerns about factual accuracy, terminology control, traceability, and checking accuracy.
   - A model's compliance claim or a programmatic check does not establish both normative compliance and factual correctness.

## Method sources and limits

[Karpathy's original post](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2105819303471976479), dated 2026-10-02 in the original record, supplied a lead for using text, diagrams, webpages, and video.

The original record states that an official X interface was used to check authorship, date, and opening text; the full long-form post was not directly obtained. Interpretation also used a [user-supplied Chinese account](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/5YPKctLVxVKXVwa1KDiH4A). Consequently, no unverified verbatim quotation is included. The post is not an ASD standard.

“80% STE” does not correspond here to a rule count, pass threshold, or measurable score. Chinese adaptation, presentation choices, and understanding checks are this skill's design. The initial version did not implement a video workflow.

## Scope of research evidence

- [Chervak, Drury, and Ouellette, 1996](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/154193129604000502): the inherited record reports 175 maintenance technicians and 16 work-card versions. It uses the checked abstract's conclusion that simplified English improved comprehension, particularly for harder material and non-native speakers. It does not invent effect sizes or extend this into a demonstrated benefit of Chinese AI explanations.
- [Wang and Friginal, 2025](https://animorepository.dlsu.edu.ph/jeal/vol4/iss2/1/): the inherited account of aviation-document corpus and paired-text computational analysis highlights cohesion when simplifying. Automated readability metrics are not this skill's users' comprehension rates and do not guarantee efficacy.

## Distribution

This package includes original synthesis, rule locations, original examples, and links. It does not distribute the complete official standard, dictionary, or translations of either, and does not present third-party open-source skills as official standards. Obtain the standard through the [official download page](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html).
