## Mental Model

**Authors' position:** the learner–world relationship is a nondual starting point for understanding learning: the internal relation between person and world is not reduced to either isolated mental constructions or an independently described world. The same object can be experienced in different ways; learning is not merely adding information to an existing container. Attend to what the object means to the learner, which aspects become focal, and which relationships remain in the background. Ways of experiencing must not simply be relabelled as internal information-processing mechanisms; neither do they invalidate other sources' study of cognitive processing. Locations: Chapters 1, 3, and 5; L870–930, L5510–5585.

**Research orientation:** phenomenography describes a limited set of qualitatively different ways of experiencing an object and their relationships. Categories of description and outcome spaces are research products, not fixed personal types or universal ability levels. Their economy, distinctions, and logical relations require examination against data. Outcome spaces are usually hierarchically related by increasing complexity, inclusiveness, or specificity; they describe collective variation represented in the sample, not an exhaustive inventory of all possible experience (PDF141–142, printed pp125–126). A second-order account concerns how an object is experienced, as distinct from a researcher's direct account of the object; this does not make facts arbitrarily relative. Locations: Chapter 6, L6160–6235, L6320–6370; end of Chapter 7 and opening of Chapter 8, L8180–8255.

## Frameworks & Structure

The eight chapters address what learning takes; qualitative differences in learning; the experience of learning; educationally critical differences in understanding the world; the anatomy of awareness; phenomenography; learning to experience; and a pedagogy of awareness. The epilogue connects discernment, simultaneity, and variation. The first half establishes the research object and its description; the second develops implications for learning and teaching. This is not a fixed instructional sequence. The authors distinguish their focal concern—qualitative changes in ways of experiencing—from learning that adds detail or develops mastery within an existing way of experiencing (PDF154–155, printed pp138–139).

## Worked Example

First identify the object, then examine what aspects the learner holds together. In a recursion problem, is it experienced as a programming construct, repetition, or self-reference? Saying “recursion” does not establish which relationship is understood. A learner might compare whether two programs express the same relationship, locate a stopping condition, or revise a program, provided sufficient programming background exists. Correct syntax and successful execution do not automatically show conceptual grasp of recursion. The three categories come from the book (PDF110, printed p94); program comparison, stopping-condition inspection, and revision are proposed teaching actions, not a reproduced research protocol. Locations: Chapter 4 review, L4175–4265; Chapter 5, L4780–4850.

The book's physics, number, and recursion materials show why quantitative answers, familiar wording, and qualitative understanding cannot simply be equated. These are particular tasks and studies; they do not show that correct action always lacks understanding. If new evidence contradicts the teaching hypothesis, revise it rather than reinterpret every success to make the diagnosis unfalsifiable.

**Book figure:** Figures 2.7 and 2.8 (printed p25, PDF41), attributed to Säljö (1982), depict different ways of understanding a particular text: thematic, hierarchical organization (D) and organization following the text’s order (S). The authors prefer the more complex D outcome in this study; S does not mean a general sequence of events. The Markdown here is badly damaged; the PDF figures were visually checked. Determine the relationship the task needs: a sequential diagram is not generally inferior to a hierarchical one, since a process may require sequence. The contrast concerns organization of an object, not the ranking of graphic appearances.

## Teaching design and evidence limits

Chapter 8's peer-teaching cases distinguish giving answers or operating the mouse for another child from helping the child grasp a relationship (L9840–9910). This directs attention to who is doing the discerning; it does not prohibit teacher explanation or demonstration. This skill consequently makes demonstrations inspectable and, when appropriate, leaves a choice or revision to the learner. Whether to invite participation still depends on the user's purpose.

Chapter 7's physics study concerns particular learners, tasks, and a short teaching period (L7800–7860). Do not combine denominators from different tables or upgrade associations into established causation. In Chapter 8's ecological-cycle comparison, classes did not receive identical visiting instruction (L9240–9310); results cannot all be attributed to one design variable. Neither study establishes one-to-one AI teaching efficacy.

Phenomenographic research requires comparison, interpretation, and examination of data. One chat message cannot yield a reliable outcome space. This skill may form revisable teaching hypotheses, but must not claim to have conducted a phenomenographic investigation or assigned a valid learner level.

## Key Takeaways

- With 4C/ID, successful whole-task performance matters but may leave an important relationship unnoticed. A meaningful change within the task can investigate this. That is cross-source synthesis, not a shared theoretical definition.
- With Alexander, genuine uptake can reveal different understandings while allowing choices and actions that are not fully verbalized.
- With multimedia research, a display can make a difference visible, but a correct answer after highlighting does not establish independent discernment. Record assistance separately.

This source does not warrant fixed learner types, ability diagnoses from one response, compulsory complete verbalization, guaranteed transfer from one contrast, a common epistemology across all five books, or claims that AI has achieved or validated “indwell.”
